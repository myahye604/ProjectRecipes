Spaghetti bolognese

Home



Description

Spaghetti Bolognese is a timeless Italian-inspired classic — tender strands of pasta tossed in a slow-cooked, deeply savory meat sauce that's rich with tomatoes, aromatics, and a touch of wine. Originating from Bologna in northern Italy, the sauce (known as ragù alla Bolognese) is all about patience and layered flavor. This is the kind of weeknight dinner that feels special — simple enough to pull together on a busy evening, yet hearty and satisfying enough to impress. Finished with freshly grated Parmesan and a crack of black pepper, it's pure comfort in a bowl.


Ingredients

  • 400g (14 oz) dried spaghetti
  • 500g (1 lb) ground beef (or a mix of beef and pork)
  • 1 medium onion, finely diced
  • 2 medium carrots, finely diced
  • 2 celery stalks, finely diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 400g (14 oz) crushed or finely chopped tomatoes
  • 120ml (½ cup) dry red wine
  • 120ml (½ cup) whole milk
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 bay leaf
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Freshly grated Parmesan, to serve
  • Fresh basil or parsley, to garnish (optional)

Steps

  1. Sauté the vegetables. Heat the olive oil in a large, heavy-bottomed pan over medium heat. Add the onion, carrot, and celery and cook for 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened and lightly golden. Add the garlic and cook for another minute.
  2. Brown the meat. Increase the heat to medium-high and add the ground meat. Break it up with a wooden spoon and cook until fully browned with no pink remaining, about 6–8 minutes. Season well with salt and pepper.
  3. Add the wine. Pour in the red wine and stir, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom of the pan. Let it simmer for 3–4 minutes until most of the wine has evaporated.
  4. Build the sauce. Stir in the tomato paste and cook for 2 minutes. Add the crushed tomatoes, oregano, and bay leaf. Stir everything together, then reduce the heat to low.
  5. Simmer low and slow. Pour in the milk (this tenderises the meat and mellows the acidity), stir, and leave the sauce to simmer uncovered for at least 30 minutes — ideally 45–60 minutes — stirring occasionally. The sauce should thicken and deepen in colour. Remove the bay leaf before serving.
  6. Cook the spaghetti. About 10 minutes before you're ready to serve, bring a large pot of well-salted water to a boil. Cook the spaghetti according to the package instructions until al dente. Reserve a cup of pasta water before draining.
  7. Combine. Add the drained spaghetti directly to the pan with the Bolognese sauce. Toss well to coat, adding a splash of reserved pasta water if needed to loosen the sauce.
  8. Serve. Divide into bowls and top with freshly grated Parmesan, a crack of black pepper, and fresh basil or parsley if using. Serve immediately.
